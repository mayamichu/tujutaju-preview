Process Communication Model

Six Personality Types.
All within each of us.

PCM describes six Personality Types, each with its own strengths, preferences and ways of connecting. We all have all six. What differs is their order and how much energy is available to us in each.

These short descriptions offer a first glimpse of what each type brings and how you can make contact.

01 / Six Personality Types

Thinker

Responsible and logical, Thinker energy values facts, information and a well considered approach.

To connect

  • Share the information they need.
  • Respect their time and recognise their work.
  • Invite their ideas.

02 / Six Personality Types

Persister

Dedicated and attentive, Persister energy brings conviction and pays close attention to what matters.

To connect

  • Ask for their opinion.
  • Make room for mutual respect.
  • Acknowledge their contribution.

03 / Six Personality Types

Harmonizer

Warm and compassionate, Harmonizer energy values a pleasant atmosphere and appreciation for the person behind the work.

To connect

  • Make the setting welcoming.
  • Express personal appreciation.
  • Invite them to collaborate with care.

04 / Six Personality Types

Rebel

Spontaneous and creative, Rebel energy brings fresh reactions, playfulness and enthusiasm for something new.

To connect

  • Make room for humour.
  • Welcome new ideas and experiences.
  • Stay open to spontaneous reactions.

05 / Six Personality Types

Promoter

Direct and charming, Promoter energy is drawn to action and finds ways to make things happen.

To connect

  • Get to the point.
  • Offer an engaging challenge.
  • Give them room to take action.

06 / Six Personality Types

Imaginer

Calm and imaginative, Imaginer energy values space to reflect and time to explore possibilities.

To connect

  • Allow time and space to think.
  • Give clear directions.
  • Invite their imagination.

One personality, six types

There are no “types of people” here.

These are Personality Types within people. Each of us has a unique Personality Structure, and no type is better than another. Recognising the differences can help us choose a way of communicating that reaches the person in front of us.

You can read more about the model on the PCM page. The descriptions here are based on the official PCM overview of the six types.
