What participants learn

Practise PCM in four steps:

Be aware of your personality

Understand your own preferences, psychological needs and the conditions that help you stay resourceful.

Recognise what others do, say and show

Learn to notice observable patterns in words, tone, behaviour and reactions instead of relying only on assumptions.

Adapt your communication style

Choose how to speak and respond so the message is easier for a different person to hear and use.

Build stronger relationships

Use those skills in everyday conversations, meetings, feedback, pressure and difficult moments.

How training works

Theory only matters
when you can use it.

01

Understand the model

Participants get a clear, practical introduction to PCM and its core concepts.

02

Connect it to real situations

Examples, discussions and exercises link the model to conversations participants already recognise from work.

03

Practise different responses

The focus is not on memorising labels. It is on noticing more and having more options when communication matters.

How teams benefit

How PCM training supports teams

Fewer conflicts

Differences are recognised earlier, before they harden into personal tension.

Greater team comfort

People have more ways to communicate without constantly pushing against each other.

Stronger mutual understanding

Team members can make more sense of behaviour that previously felt confusing or frustrating.

More balanced participation

Different communication styles have more room to contribute.

Stronger team stability

A shared framework makes difficult periods easier to navigate together.

Increased satisfaction

People are more likely to feel understood, respected and able to work in ways that suit them.
